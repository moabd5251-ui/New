# Year One — Two Vehicles, Private Pay, King County

The operating plan. One correction to the premise first, because it changes the timeline
and it's better to know now than in month eleven.

---

## The premise needs fixing: two vehicles doesn't unlock Hopelink

Hopelink's Partner Application sets **two independent minimums**:

1. A minimum of **one year of NEMT experience**
2. A vehicle fleet of **five or more vehicles**

A two-vehicle year gets you the first and leaves you **three vehicles short of the second**.
There is no year-two Hopelink contract at the end of this plan. Realistically you're looking
at **year three or four** to reach a five-vehicle fleet, and only if the private-pay book
grows fast enough to fund vehicles three, four, and five.

**One question worth asking them directly, because it could change everything:** does the
five-vehicle count include *subcontracted or leased* vehicles operating under your authority,
or only vehicles you own? Some brokers count capacity, not title. If capacity counts, a
network of owner-operators driving under your contract could reach five far faster than
buying four more cars. If it must be owned vehicles, you're on the slow path. **Ask
transportationcontracts@hopelink.org this specific question** — it's the difference between
a three-year plan and a six-year one, and nobody but them can answer it.

---

## The reframe that makes the plan work anyway

**Stop treating Hopelink as the goal.** Run the numbers honestly and the target is
questionable:

| | Net margin | Payment terms | Concentration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hopelink / Medicaid | 8–15% | 30–90 days | One buyer, re-biddable |
| Private pay & facility-direct | 20–35% | Your terms | Many small contracts |

You'd be spending three years and four vehicles to earn the right to lower-margin, slower-paying,
more concentrated revenue. **That's an expansion option, not a destination.**

The right year-one goal is **a profitable two-vehicle private-pay book that doesn't need
Hopelink at all.** If it works, Hopelink becomes a volume-filler you can take or leave — and
you'll negotiate from a much better position as a profitable operator with a safety record
than as a startup that needs them. If private pay doesn't work, Hopelink was never going to
rescue it: 8–15% margins don't save a business that can't clear 25%.

So: build the book. Keep the records clean enough to qualify later. Decide about Hopelink in
year three from a position of strength.

## What two vehicles actually costs

The jump from one vehicle to two is not 2× — it's more, because the second vehicle needs a
**paid driver**, and that brings payroll, workers' comp, and unemployment insurance with it.

### Capital gate, two vehicles

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Insurance down payment — vehicle 1 (20–35% of $5.5–9k) | $900–$3,200 |
| Insurance down payment — vehicle 2 | $900–$3,200 |
| Second vehicle, used, ambulatory-eligible (under 8 yrs) | $8,000–$18,000 (or financed) |
| WA Business License Application | $90 |
| Seattle BLTC (new business, Tier 1) | $73 |
| Equipment, both vehicles | $400–$800 |
| Payroll setup, L&I and ESD accounts | $0 (included in the license application) |
| **Two-vehicle gate** | **≈ $10,400–$25,400** with a purchased second vehicle |
| **If you finance vehicle 2** | **≈ $4,400–$9,400** down, plus a monthly note |

Against a starting position of no capital and no runway, **that is a materially different
mountain** than the $1,300–$3,800 single-vehicle gate. It is reachable, but not in the same
timeframe, and not without the Stage 1 job.

### Monthly fixed cost, two vehicles

| | Monthly |
|---|---|
| Insurance, 2 vehicles | $920–$1,500 |
| Vehicle note (if financed) | $250–$450 |
| Dispatch software | $50–$200 |
| Phone, accounting, licenses | $110–$200 |
| **Fixed nut before a single trip** | **$1,330–$2,350** |

Plus B&O on gross, owed even in a losing month. **Two vehicles roughly doubles the monthly
number you must clear before you earn a dollar** — which is why the sequencing below matters
more than anything else in this document.

## Sequencing: launch one, add the second on a trigger

You asked for two vehicles. Here's the version that gets you there without betting the whole
thing on month one.

**Launch with vehicle one. Add vehicle two when a specific trigger fires**, not on a date:

> **Trigger:** vehicle one is running **70+ trips/month for two consecutive months** and you
> are turning down or rescheduling trips you could have served.

Turned-down trips are the only honest signal that capacity is the constraint. Adding a
second van and a driver before that signal means paying $460–$750/month in insurance plus a
wage to chase demand you haven't proven exists.

Why this still gets you to two vehicles inside year one: at Washington rates, a single van
clearing 70 trips/month produces roughly $5,600 in revenue and $3,300–$4,300 in owner
take-home. **Two to three months at that level funds vehicle two's down payment from
operations** rather than from savings you don't have. You end year one with two vehicles and
a documented year — same destination, dramatically less risk of not getting there.

If you launch both simultaneously, you carry a doubled fixed nut through the validation
period, when your book is smallest and least certain. With no runway, that's the version
most likely to end at zero.

## Hiring the second driver — Washington specifics

**Your driver is a W-2 employee, not a 1099 contractor.** Washington applies the **ABC test**,
which *presumes employment* unless you can prove all three prongs — including that the worker
is free from your direction and control both by contract *and in practice*. A driver running
your routes, in your vehicle, to your schedule, under your contracts fails that on its face.
L&I also applies an economic-realities test and expects you to document how each contractor
passes every part. Misclassification penalties in Washington are significant, and the
exposure includes back premiums, benefits, and unemployment insurance.

Don't be clever here. Hire W-2.

**Workers' comp works differently in Washington than most states.** L&I is a state-run
monopoly fund, and **premiums are assessed per hour worked, not as a percentage of payroll.**
Average rates rose 4.9% for 2026. Look up your specific risk classification and hourly rate
at `lni.wa.gov/WorkersCompRates` before you hire — transportation classes are not cheap, and
the hours-based structure means a part-time driver costs proportionally the same as a
full-time one.

The Business License Application you already filed sets up both your L&I and Employment
Security accounts, so the administrative lift is small. The cost is not.

**Budget roughly a 20% burden on top of the hourly wage** for payroll taxes and workers'
comp — that's what the calculator applies.

## Making the year *provable*

The one-year experience requirement only counts if you can document it. Hopelink will not
take your word for it in year three, and neither will an insurer pricing your second-year
premium or a buyer valuing the business later. **Start the records on day one** — reconstructing
them later is impossible.

Keep, from the first trip:

- **Trip logs** — date, passenger (or de-identified ID), pickup, destination, mileage, times,
  driver. Your dispatch software should do this; make sure it exports.
- **Insurance loss runs** — a clean two-year loss history is what drops your premium from
  first-year-surcharge pricing to established-operator pricing. It's worth real money.
- **Signed facility contracts and dated invoices** — proof of commercial operation, not just
  activity.
- **Driver credential files** — licenses, MVRs, background checks, CPR/First Aid, PASS or
  equivalent training, with expiration dates calendared.
- **Vehicle maintenance and inspection records** per vehicle.
- **Safety record** — incidents, complaints, and how each was resolved. A clean, *documented*
  record is the single most persuasive thing you can put in front of a broker.
- **Corporate records** — separate bank account, clean books, B&O filings, annual report.

This is the unglamorous half of the plan and the half that determines whether year three
happens.

## The twelve months

| Months | Focus | Milestone |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-1** | Stage 0 validation calls; Stage 1 driver job at an established King County operator | 3+ facilities reporting unreliable incumbents; gate money accumulating from wages |
| **1–2** | Entity, licenses, insurance quotes, first facility contract signed *before* binding insurance | Vehicle 1 legal and insured with revenue already committed |
| **3–5** | Operate nights/weekends around the day job. Ambulatory only. Dialysis + assisted living | 40–60 trips/month, breakeven cleared |
| **6–8** | Go full-time on the business if trips justify it. Push toward the 70-trip trigger | 70+ trips/month, turning work away |
| **9–10** | **Trigger fires:** finance vehicle 2, hire the W-2 driver, L&I active | 2 vehicles running, ~110–140 trips/month |
| **11–12** | Stabilize. Clean books, clean loss run, documented year. Re-contact Hopelink | One documented year of NEMT operation; plan vehicles 3–5 |

**Rough year-one economics** if the trigger fires around month nine: roughly $45,000–$60,000
in revenue, with owner take-home in the $28,000–$40,000 range — most of it your own driving
wage rather than profit. That is **not** a replacement for a union transit paycheck in year
one. It becomes one in years two and three, when the second and third vehicles run without you.

Go in knowing that. The first year of this pays you like a job with extra paperwork; the
value is the book, the record, and the vehicles you own at the end of it.

## Ask Hopelink these four questions now

Not to apply — to plan. Email **transportationcontracts@hopelink.org**, call **1-800-923-7433**.

1. Does the five-vehicle minimum count **owned vehicles only**, or does it include leased or
   subcontracted vehicles operating under our authority?
2. Does the one-year NEMT experience requirement count **private-pay operation**, or only
   Medicaid/brokered trips?
3. What documentation do you require to evidence that year — trip logs, loss runs, contracts?
4. Are you currently adding providers in King County, and do you expect to be in 2027–28?

Question 2 is the one that could quietly sink this plan: **if only brokered trips count as
"NEMT experience," a private-pay year may not qualify at all.** That is exactly the kind of
thing published requirements don't spell out and a five-minute call resolves. Ask it before
you build a three-year plan on the assumption.
