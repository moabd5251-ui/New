Earnings Drift

Trade with the gap, after the report · low-vega structures only
Updated 04 Aug 2026, 13:03 UTC
The premise: a stock that gaps on an earnings surprise tends to keep travelling that way for days to weeks rather than repricing instantly. The effect is strongest in the first week, fades over a couple of months, and is far more reliable when heavy volume confirmed the gap. What kills it is the gap filling — price back through the pre-report close means the market rejected the surprise and there is nothing left to ride.
The trap this avoids: implied volatility collapses the instant the report lands. Buy a call after a beat and the crush routinely eats more than the direction earns — you are right about the stock and still lose money. Every structure below is deliberately low-vega: shares, deep in-the-money options that behave like shares, or debit spreads whose short leg cancels most of what is left. Long at-the-money options are the obvious instrument here and the wrong one.

How this is measured

The reaction bar is found by scanning for the largest opening gap within three days of the reported date, then measured two ways: the gap itself (open against the prior close) and the full-day move (close against prior close). They diverge when the gap gets bought or sold into, which is itself informative.

Volume confirmation compares reaction-day volume to its trailing 21-day average. Drift on heavy volume reflects real repositioning; on light volume it is closer to noise, and those names are demoted regardless of gap size.

Gap fill is the disqualifier. If any bar since the reaction has traded back through the pre-report close, the surprise has been rejected and the setup is dropped no matter how large the original move.

Stops and targets come from the same confluence engine as the other dashboards — stop behind the nearest swing beyond the reaction, target at the next level in the drift direction. Where that target sits close, the card says so rather than presenting a thin trade as a clean one.

Deep in-the-money strikes are chosen around 80 delta: they track the stock closely, cost a fraction of the shares, and carry little remaining volatility exposure to lose. The capital figure on each card is what the option costs relative to buying the stock outright.

The window is twelve days. Drift is measurable for far longer in the literature, but it is strongest immediately, and anything past the first week here is demoted rather than recommended.

Analysis only. Nothing here is placed, ordered, or executed — and none of it is financial advice. The backtest above has a real weakness worth naming: historical announcement dates are not available on this feed, so reactions were inferred from outsized gaps on heavy volume. Any non-earnings shock on high volume was counted as a report. Gap and volume figures on the live cards come from completed daily bars and are solid; option prices are delayed and go stale outside market hours, which the cards flag. Verify against a live book before acting.
