# King County — The Gate Nobody Mentions

Your county's broker is **Hopelink** (King and Snohomish, 1-800-923-7433), the HCA-contracted
NEMT broker since 1990. This file is the most specific layer in the set, and it contains one
finding that changes the shape of the whole plan.

---

## The finding: Hopelink's door is closed to a startup

Hopelink's own Partner Application for Transportation Service sets two minimums for
contracted providers:

> **A minimum of one year of NEMT experience**, plus all required Washington licensing.
> **A vehicle fleet of five or more vehicles.**

Review takes up to 30 days. Contact is **transportationcontracts@hopelink.org**.

**Read that plainly: you cannot start with Hopelink.** Not "it's competitive" — you are
ineligible on day one, on two independent criteria, and no amount of capital fixes the
experience requirement. A single-van startup does not meet either bar.

Confirm it yourself on the call — published requirements go stale and exceptions sometimes
exist — but **plan as though it's true**, because two independent readings of their
application say the same thing.

---

## Why this is survivable, and arguably good

Everything the earlier documents said about broker work being the low-margin trap is still
true — 8–15% net, 30–90 day payment lag, and concentration risk against a single buyer.
King County simply removes that option at the start, which forces you into the half of the
business that was always better:

**Your King County business is private-pay and facility-direct, from day one.** 20–35% net
margins. Payment on your terms, not in 90 days. No broker who can re-bid you away. At
Washington's rates — $45–$75 base plus $2.50–$4.50 per mile ambulatory — that's a real
business without touching Medicaid at all.

The constraint also clarifies the sequence. Stage 1 in `00-START-HERE.md` — going to drive
for an existing NEMT provider — was optional capital formation. **In King County it is
mandatory and strategic**, because it is how you accumulate the year of NEMT experience
Hopelink requires. That job is no longer a detour on the way to your business; it is the
first requirement of it.

---

## The King County market looks under-served

Directory data for Seattle shows roughly **five providers listing service-area coverage** —
one based inside the city, four serving it from nearby bases — with five listed for
ambulatory and five for dialysis, in a county of 2.3 million people. That is thin.

Supporting signal: in a February 2026 survey of healthcare professionals, more than half
estimated that **11–20% of missed appointments trace directly to transportation barriers**.

Don't take either number as proof. They're a reason to make the Stage 0 calls, not a
substitute for making them. But the thesis you're testing — that King County facilities are
under-served on reliable ambulatory transport — has directional support before you dial.

## Revised plan for King County

| Stage | What changes here |
|---|---|
| **0 — Validate** | Unchanged, and now more important: private pay is your *only* channel, so facility demand is the whole business case, not half of it. |
| **1 — Get inside** | **Now mandatory.** Drive for an established King County NEMT provider. You are buying the year of experience Hopelink requires *and* the gate money *and* the local knowledge, on someone else's payroll. |
| **2 — Register & insure** | Same stack, minus broker enrollment. Skip ModivCare-style credentialing entirely — it doesn't apply here. |
| **3 — Operate** | Facility contracts and private pay only. Dialysis centers, assisted living, adult day health, discharge rides. |
| **4 — Hopelink, years 2–5** | Once you have a year of documented NEMT operation and are building toward five vehicles, Hopelink becomes an expansion channel rather than a starting one. |

That last row is the honest long game. Five vehicles is a multi-year build, not a plan for
this year — and by the time you get there, the Medicaid volume is a supplement to a
profitable private-pay book rather than the foundation of a fragile one. That's a better
business than the one you'd have built starting the other way around.

## King County / Seattle costs

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| WA Business License Application (state, incl. UBI) | **$90** |
| Seattle Business License Tax Certificate — new business, Tier 1 | **$73**, or **$37** if you start July 1 or later |
| City license if outside Seattle (Bellevue, Kent, Renton, Auburn, Federal Way…) | Varies — check your specific city |
| Insurance down payment (20–35% of premium) | $900–$3,200 |
| Vehicle equipment | $200–$400 |
| **King County gate** | **≈ $1,300–$3,800** |

Note the Seattle figure carefully: the **$667** you'll see quoted is a high-revenue tier
based on the prior complete calendar year. A new business defaults to Tier 1. Don't let the
big number scare you off, and don't budget the small one forever — it scales with revenue.

**One open question to resolve by phone.** Seattle administers for-hire *vehicle* licensing
for both the city and King County, while the County administers *driver* regulations. Whether
ambulatory NEMT falls under for-hire vehicle licensing, or sits outside it as a medical
transport service, is not something I could establish from public sources. Call **Seattle
Consumer Protection** and **King County Records and Licensing** and ask directly. If it does
apply, it adds licensing cost and a vehicle inspection regime — worth knowing before you
bind insurance, not after.

## Revised first week

1. **Email transportationcontracts@hopelink.org and call 1-800-923-7433.** Confirm the
   one-year experience and five-vehicle minimums, and ask what would make you eligible later.
   You're not applying — you're getting the timeline for year three. *(20 minutes)*
2. **Start applying to King County NEMT providers as a driver.** This is now the critical
   path, not a side quest — it's the only way to accumulate the experience Hopelink requires,
   and it pays you while you do it. Care Bridge Medical Transport and the other established
   King County operators are where to start.
3. **Stage 0 calls to dialysis centers.** DaVita and Fresenius both run multiple King County
   clinics; each has its own social worker. Scripts in `01-VALIDATION-SCRIPTS.md`.
4. **Call Seattle Consumer Protection and King County Records & Licensing** about for-hire
   vehicle licensing for medical transport. *(20 minutes)*
5. **Call WA Department of Revenue** about your B&O classification and rate. *(15 minutes)*
6. **Check your vehicle's year and mileage**, and open the gate savings account.

Five of those six cost nothing but phone time, and the sixth is a savings transfer.
