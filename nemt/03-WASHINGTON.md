# Washington State — Specifics That Change the Plan

State-specific layer over `00-START-HERE.md`. Two things here are **corrections** to the
national guidance in the earlier files, not additions. August 2026.

---

## Correction 1 — Washington does not use the national brokers

The earlier documents told you to enroll with ModivCare, MTM, Veyo, SafeRide, or
Access2Care. **That is wrong for Washington.** Those companies do not run Washington's
Medicaid NEMT.

Since 1989 Washington has used a **regional brokerage model** administered by the **Health
Care Authority (HCA)**. The state is divided among **six regional brokers — all nonprofits
or public agencies**, each holding an exclusive territory. You contract with **the broker
for your county**, and there is exactly one.

| Regional broker | Counties | Phone |
|---|---|---|
| **Hopelink** | King, Snohomish | 1-800-923-7433 |
| **Paratransit Services** | Clallam, Grays Harbor, Jefferson, Kitsap, Lewis, Mason, Pacific, Pierce, Thurston | 1-800-756-5438 |
| **Northwest Regional Council** | Island, San Juan, Skagit, Whatcom | 1-800-860-6812 |
| **Community in Motion** (Human Services Council) | Clark, Cowlitz, Klickitat, Skamania, Wahkiakum | 1-800-752-9422 |
| **People For People** | Benton, Chelan, Columbia, Douglas, Franklin, Kittitas, Okanogan, Walla Walla, Yakima | 1-800-233-1624 |
| **Special Mobility Services** | Eastern Washington, including Spokane and Stevens | — |

**Why this is better news than it sounds.** These are mission-driven nonprofits and public
agencies, not national for-profit brokers optimizing a margin against you. There is one
door to knock on rather than five, and the relationship is local — the person deciding
whether to add you as a subcontracted provider works in your region.

**Why it's also a risk.** One broker per territory means one customer for all your Medicaid
volume. If they aren't adding providers in your county, that channel is simply closed until
they are. **This is the single most important call you make** — it determines whether the
broker half of your plan exists at all, and it is free.

To serve Medicaid clients you must also be **certified by HCA**, in addition to holding the
broker subcontract.

## Correction 2 — Washington is cheap to license, but taxes your gross

**The good news: no Certificate of Need.** Washington's CON program covers hospitals,
nursing home beds, hospice, and kidney disease treatment centers. **NEMT is not subject to
CON** — it's regulated through HCA and the Medicaid program instead. Incumbents cannot block
your entry. That was the check that could have ended this plan, and it comes back clear.

**Licensing is unusually cheap.** The Washington Business License Application through the
Department of Revenue is a **$90 state filing fee**, and the same application registers your
UBI number, state tax accounts, city endorsements, and your L&I and Employment Security
accounts. Seattle, Tacoma, and Spokane add city endorsement fees on top. Compare that to the
$1,000–$5,000 national average for licensing — Washington is at the very bottom of the range.

**The offset: B&O tax.** Washington has no personal income tax, but it charges Business &
Occupation tax on **gross receipts**, with **no deduction for fuel, insurance, wages, or any
other expense.** You owe B&O on every dollar of revenue in a losing month as readily as a
good one. The calculator now includes it as a line item at a 1.5% placeholder — **confirm
your actual rate and classification with the Department of Revenue**, because transportation
activities can fall under a different rate than general services, and getting classified
wrong is a back-taxes problem rather than a rounding error.

## Washington rates are above the national average

Published 2026 Washington private-pay ranges:

| Service | Base per trip | Per mile |
|---|---|---|
| Ambulatory (sedan) | **$45–$75** | **$2.50–$4.50** |
| Wheelchair | $75–$140 | $3.50–$7.00 |
| Stretcher | $350–$625 | $7.00–$16.00 |

Common surcharges in this market: weekends 25–50%, holidays 50–100%, stair assistance
$25–$50 per flight, oxygen $25–$40, after-hours $10–$25.

The calculator now defaults to $55 base and $3.25/mile — mid-range for Washington ambulatory
work, and meaningfully better than the national figures it shipped with. **Broker rates in
the calculator remain a placeholder**: your regional broker sets those, and only they can
tell you the real number. Ask on the first call.

## Insurance in Washington

Same structural picture as the national guidance, with one local note: **HCA-contracted
brokers push $1.5M combined single limit on stretcher work.** Ambulatory requirements are
lower — confirm the exact minimum with your regional broker rather than guessing, since it
directly sets your premium.

Washington-wide, NEMT commercial auto runs roughly **$3,000–$12,000 per vehicle per year**
depending on vehicle type and whether you carry wheelchair or stretcher passengers. An
ambulatory-only sedan or minivan sits at the low end of that band. **Down payment at binding
is still 20–35%.**

## Revised capital gate — Washington, ambulatory, vehicle you own

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Insurance down payment (20–35% of premium) | $900–$3,200 |
| WA Business License Application | **$90** |
| City endorsement (Seattle / Tacoma / Spokane) | $0–$150 |
| USDOT number *(confirm whether intrastate-only requires one)* | $0–$300 |
| NPI, EIN, UBI | **$0** |
| Required vehicle equipment | $200–$400 |
| **Washington gate** | **≈ $1,200–$4,100** |

That is **lower than the $3,000–$8,000 national estimate** in `00-START-HERE.md`, driven
almost entirely by the $90 license fee. Washington is one of the cheaper states in the
country to open this business — the barrier here really is just insurance.

## When you hire

The same Business License Application sets up your **L&I workers' compensation** and
**Employment Security** accounts. Washington L&I is a state-run monopoly fund for workers'
comp, not a private market, and transportation risk classes are not cheap. Budget for it
before you hire your first driver — it's part of why the calculator adds a 20% burden on top
of the wage you enter.

## Revised first week, Washington version

1. **Find your county in the table above and call that broker.** Ask: are you accepting new
   subcontracted transportation providers in my county; what are your insurance minimums for
   ambulatory; what are your rates; what does credentialing involve and how long does it
   take. **This one call decides whether the broker channel is open to you.** *(20 minutes)*
2. **Check your vehicle's model year and mileage.** Still the fact that swings your gate the
   most. *(5 minutes)*
3. **Pick 8 dialysis centers within 30 minutes** and start the Stage 0 calls in
   `01-VALIDATION-SCRIPTS.md`. Washington's private-pay rates make this the high-margin half
   of the business — $55 base plus $3.25/mile beats broker reimbursement by a wide margin.
4. **Call the Department of Revenue** and ask which B&O classification NEMT falls under and
   at what rate. Free, and it changes your model. *(15 minutes)*
5. **Open the gate savings account** and set the automatic transfer. At $1,200–$4,100,
   Washington's gate is reachable in **2–5 months** of disciplined saving from overtime —
   materially faster than the national estimate assumed.
