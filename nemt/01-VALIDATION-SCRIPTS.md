# Stage 0 — Free Validation Scripts

**Cost: $0. Time: about 6 hours over four weeks. Purpose: find out whether unmet demand
exists before you spend a cent.**

You are not selling. You have nothing to sell yet. You are a person with a commercial
passenger license doing research on whether a route is worth running. That framing is
true, it's disarming, and it gets people talking.

---

## Who to call, in priority order

| Target | Why | How many to call |
|---|---|---|
| **Dialysis centers** | Patients travel 3x/week, indefinitely. Recurring revenue, predictable schedule, and they suffer most from unreliable transport. | 8 |
| **Assisted living / memory care** | Frequent appointments, staff who personally deal with transport failures. | 5 |
| **Adult day health centers** | Daily round trips, often contracted in blocks. | 3 |
| **Outpatient surgery centers** | Discharge rules require an escort — they cannot release a patient without a ride. | 3 |
| **Oncology infusion centers** | Recurring, scheduled, medically fragile. | 3 |

Find them: search "dialysis center near [your city]" — DaVita and Fresenius dominate and
each clinic has its own social worker. **The social worker or case manager is who you
want**, not the front desk and not the clinic manager. They are the person who personally
absorbs the pain when a patient's ride doesn't show, and they are rarely gatekept.

Call between **10am and 3pm**, Tuesday through Thursday. Avoid Monday mornings and Friday
afternoons — those are the busiest and most stressed hours in any clinic.

## Script 1 — Dialysis center (your primary target)

> "Hi, I'm hoping to reach the social worker or whoever handles patient transportation.
> — Hi, my name's [name]. I'm not selling anything and I'm not with a vendor. I drive
> commercially here in [area] and I'm looking into whether there's actually a need for more
> medical transport around here before I go any further with it. Could I ask you three quick
> questions about how transport works for your patients?"

Then, exactly these three:

1. **"Who do your patients use for rides right now?"**
2. **"On a typical week, how often does a patient miss or get late to a treatment because
   the ride didn't show?"**
3. **"If you could change one thing about how patient transport works here, what would it
   be?"**

Then stop talking. The third question is where the real answer lives, and people fill
silence with the truth.

**If they describe problems, one follow-up:**

> "That's helpful, thank you. If I do end up running this, would it be alright if I checked
> back with you in a few months? And roughly what does a one-way trip run around here — I'm
> trying to figure out if the numbers even work."

That last question gets you local rate data for free, from the buyer's side. It's the most
valuable sentence in this document.

## Script 2 — Assisted living / adult day health

> "Hi — I'm looking into starting a medical transport service in [area] and I'm trying to
> find out whether there's a real need before I invest in it. Do you arrange transportation
> for residents to appointments, or do families handle that themselves?"

Follow-ups:
1. "When you do arrange it, who do you call?"
2. "Are they reliable? Do they ever turn down short-notice trips?"
3. "Do you have a contract with them, or is it trip by trip?"

**"Trip by trip" is the answer you're hoping for** — no contract means no switching cost,
and that's a door you can walk through later.

## Script 3 — Insurance reality check (make this call in week 3)

This is the only Stage 0 call that isn't about demand. It converts the biggest unknown in
your plan into a real number, and quotes are free.

Call two or three commercial insurance brokers who write NEMT. Say:

> "I'm looking at starting a non-emergency medical transport operation, ambulatory only —
> no wheelchair lift — one vehicle, one driver, that's me. I'd be running in [state]. I have
> a commercial license with a passenger endorsement and a clean record, but no prior
> commercial auto loss history. Can you give me a rough annual premium and what the down
> payment at binding would look like?"

Have ready: vehicle year/make/model and mileage, your license class and endorsements, your
MVR history, and estimated annual mileage.

**What you're listening for:** annual premium and the percentage due at binding. Expect
$5,500–$9,000/year for a sedan or minivan with $1M auto liability, plus the 30–50%
first-year surcharge, and 20–35% due up front. If your quote comes back far above that,
that's your answer about this market, and it cost you nothing but a phone call.

## Script 4 — Broker landscape (week 4)

Call or check the provider-enrollment pages for the brokers operating in your state —
ModivCare, MTM, Veyo, SafeRide Health, Access2Care. Ask:

1. "Are you currently accepting new transportation providers in [county]?"
2. "What are your insurance minimums?" *(expect $1M auto liability; SafeRide has been lower
   at $500k)*
3. "What's the vehicle age limit?" *(ModivCare: under 8 years at enrollment, hard exit at 10)*
4. "How long does credentialing take, start to finish?"
5. "What are your payment terms?"

"We're not accepting new providers in your county" is genuinely useful — it means either
the market is saturated or the broker is locked up with an incumbent, and both change your
plan. Better to learn it now than after you've bought insurance.

## Go / no-go, decided in advance

Write the decision rule down now, before the calls, so you judge the results rather than
rationalize them.

**GO — proceed to Stage 1** if all three are true:
- **3 or more facilities** report their current provider is late, cancels, or refuses
  short-notice trips
- **2 or more** said yes to a callback
- Local one-way rates you heard support the math in the calculator at your realistic volume

**NO-GO — stop, having spent $0** if:
- Facilities are satisfied with existing providers, or
- Everyone is locked into contracts with no complaints, or
- Your insurance quote makes the unit economics negative at achievable volume

A no-go here is a **successful outcome**, not a failure. It cost you six hours instead of
eight thousand dollars, and it points you back to the wage moves in
`../BUS_OPERATOR_PLAYBOOK.md` — which, at zero runway, were always the safer play anyway.

## Two rules while calling

**Never imply you're already operating.** You're researching. Saying otherwise to a
healthcare facility about patient transport is the kind of misrepresentation that follows
you in a small local market where every social worker knows the others.

**Log everything the day you hear it.** In six months these notes are your entire sales
pipeline — the callback list, the pricing intelligence, and the names of the people who
already told you what's broken. That's worth more than anything you could buy.
