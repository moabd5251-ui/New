Built for the actual constraint: **no capital, no runway, keep the paycheck.**

## The one number that decides everything

The internet says a one-vehicle NEMT startup costs **$30,000–$60,000**. That figure
assumes you buy a wheelchair-accessible van. It is the single biggest reason people give
up on this before starting, and **it does not apply to your entry point.**

Here's the real gate for an **ambulatory-only** start using a vehicle you already own:

| Item | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial auto insurance — **down payment** | **$1,600–$3,150** | 20–35% of premium due at binding, then monthly |
| (Annual premium, sedan/minivan) | ($5,500–$9,000) | New operators pay 30–50% over established rates |
| LLC registration | $50–$500 | State-dependent |
| USDOT number | $300 | Required in most states |
| NPI number | **$0** | Free, always |
| State NEMT operating license | $0–$1,500 | Varies wildly by state |
| Required equipment (first aid kit, fire extinguisher, spill kit, seat belt cutter) | $200–$400 | Broker-specified lists |
| **Realistic cash gate** | **≈ $3,000–$8,000** | Assumes you own a qualifying vehicle |

**The vehicle is the variable that decides which number you're facing.** If you own a
vehicle under 8 years old with reasonable mileage, you may be near the bottom of that
range. If you need to buy one, you're at the top or beyond. Check your own vehicle's year
first — that single fact moves the gate by tens of thousands.

**Insurance is the real barrier, not the van.** Premiums are running 12–15% above 2024
because carriers are exiting the NEMT space, and first-year operators with no loss history
pay a 30–50% surcharge on top. Nobody waives this, and you cannot legally carry patients
without it. Anyone telling you to start NEMT with $0 is either ignoring insurance or
telling you to commit fraud by hauling patients on a personal auto policy — which voids
the policy the moment a claim happens and ends with a judgment against you personally.

So: there is a gate, it's real, and it's roughly $3k–$8k rather than $50k. The plan below
is how to walk through it without borrowing.

## The four stages

### Stage 0 — Validate for free (weeks 1–4) · **cost: $0**

Do not register anything. Do not call an insurance broker for a policy. Do not look at
vans. You are answering one question: **does unmet demand exist within 30 minutes of you?**

Work the call scripts in `01-VALIDATION-SCRIPTS.md`. Targets: dialysis centers first
(patients ride three times a week indefinitely — the closest thing to recurring revenue in
this business), then assisted living, adult day health, outpatient surgery, and oncology
infusion centers.

**Go/no-go:** you need **three or more facilities** telling you their current provider is
late, cancels, or won't take short-notice trips. Three complaints is a business. Zero
complaints means your market is served and you should stop here — having spent nothing.

### Stage 1 — Get inside the industry, funded (months 1–6) · **cost: $0, income: positive**

This is the part most guides skip and it's the highest-leverage move you have.

**Go drive for an existing NEMT provider.** W-2, using their vehicle and their insurance.
Your passenger-endorsed CDL and clean record make you a strong hire — you're overqualified
for the role. You get paid while you learn the broker landscape, trip pricing, scheduling
software, and which local operators are failing the facilities you called in Stage 0. You
also learn whether you actually like this work before you bet on it.

**Simultaneously, this is your capital-formation engine.** The $3k–$8k gate gets funded
from overtime and this job, not from a loan. At $150–$250/week set aside, the gate is
cleared in 4–8 months. That is the entire "no capital" problem, solved by sequencing
rather than borrowing.

> If you're currently union transit: don't quit for this. Stay, take overtime, and bank the
> gate money faster than the NEMT driver job would produce it. The pension math in
> `../BUS_OPERATOR_PLAYBOOK.md` doesn't change just because the goal is now a business.

### Stage 2 — Register, insure, contract (month 6–9) · **cost: the gate**

Only once Stage 0 found demand and Stage 1 funded the gate. Full ordered checklist with
costs in `02-COMPLIANCE-AND-COSTS.md`. Summary of the order that matters:

1. LLC → EIN (free) → NPI (free)
2. Get **insurance quotes before committing to anything else** — this is the number that
   can kill the plan, so find it out before you've spent on licensing
3. USDOT number, state NEMT operating license, city/county business license
4. Broker enrollment (ModivCare, MTM, Veyo, SafeRide, Access2Care) — they require
   background checks via Checkr, OIG LEIE and NSOPW clearance, current CPR and First Aid,
   and PASS or equivalent training. Start CPR/First Aid early; it's cheap and it expires.
5. Sign your first private-pay facility contract from Stage 0

### Stage 3 — Run ambulatory, then add capacity (month 9+)

Start ambulatory-only, evenings and weekends, keeping the day job. Chase **private pay and
direct facility contracts over Medicaid broker trips** — that's the difference between
8–15% and 20–35% net margin. Add a wheelchair-accessible vehicle only when signed contract
volume justifies financing one. Then a second driver. Then you're out of the van and it's
a business rather than a job.

## Why broker trips are the low-margin trap

Two revenue streams, wildly different economics:

| | Rate | Net margin | Cash timing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Medicaid broker** (ModivCare, MTM, Veyo) | $25–$90 base + $0.70–$10.00/loaded mile | **8–15%** | 30–90 days |
| **Private pay / facility direct** | $35–$80 ambulatory base + $2.00–$3.50/mile; wheelchair $65–$145 + $3.00–$5.50/mile | **20–35%** | Immediate or net-15 |

Broker work is worth having for volume and predictable dispatch — it fills the schedule.
But a book that's all broker trips nets 8–15% on 30–90 day payment terms, which is a
cash-flow trap for someone with no cushion. **Facility and private-pay contracts are the
business; broker trips are the filler.** Build the ratio deliberately from day one.

Rates vary a lot by state (California ambulatory runs $45–$80 base; New Mexico $30–$55), so
use the calculator with your own state's numbers rather than these.

## Honest risk list

- **Insurance is a recurring, unavoidable $5,500–$9,000/year** whether you run 10 trips or
  200. It's a fixed cost that doesn't care about your volume — meaning low volume doesn't
  just earn less, it loses money.
- **Broker contracts get re-bid and can be pulled.** Concentration in one broker is the
  same risk as one customer.
- **Broker credentialing is strict and expires.** ModivCare deactivates drivers whose
  background screening is more than 365 days old, requires vehicles under 8 years old at
  enrollment with a hard exit at 10 years, and 200,000+ miles draws scrutiny. Missing a
  documentation deadline gets you de-fleeted regardless of your compliance standing.
- **You are transporting medically vulnerable people.** The liability is real, which is
  precisely why the insurance costs what it does.
- **The online NEMT advice ecosystem is heavily monetized.** Anyone selling you a course or
  a "provider startup package" is making money from the course, not from running vans.
  Everything in these documents is available free from your state Medicaid office and the
  brokers' own provider-enrollment pages.

## Do this in the next 7 days

1. Check your vehicle's model year and mileage — this decides your gate. *(5 minutes)*
2. Pick 8 dialysis centers within 30 minutes of home. Their patients are your recurring
   revenue. *(20 minutes)*
3. Make the first three validation calls from `01-VALIDATION-SCRIPTS.md`. *(1 hour)*
4. Open a separate savings account and set the automatic transfer for the gate. *(15 minutes)*
5. Search "[your state] non-emergency medical transportation provider enrollment" and read
   your state's actual requirements — free, authoritative, and the only version that
   binds you. *(30 minutes)*

Nothing above costs a dollar. That's the point.
