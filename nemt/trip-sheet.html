Volume

One-way trips. A dialysis patient riding three times a week is six one-way trips. Count hours door to door — pickup wait, loading, the drive, unloading, and the empty miles to the next pickup. That last part is why 1.25 hours, not 0.5.

What you charge

Washington's published ambulatory range is $45–$75 base and $2.50–$4.50/mile — but that's retail private pay. Facilities negotiate volume down, so these defaults sit near the bottom of the range. Quote retail to individuals; expect to discount for a dialysis center sending you six trips a week. Broker rates below only matter from year three, once Hopelink is reachable.

What it costs

Insurance is the fixed cost that doesn't care about your volume. First-year operators pay 30–50% over established rates — no loss history.

Washington charges B&O on gross receipts — no deduction for fuel, insurance, or wages. You owe it in a losing month too. Confirm your rate and classification with the Department of Revenue; transportation can fall under a different rate than general services.
