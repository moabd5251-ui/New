Excess is interest relative to what this chain carries at that distance — volume and gamma both peak at the money by construction, so raw size just rediscovers spot. A strike scores here by punching above the decay curve, which is what makes it somewhere price is not yet.

Flow bias: PUTS — downside flow dominates Downside flow 77,576 contracts against upside 60,088 — ratio 1.29. Puts bought below spot force dealers to SELL the underlying as it falls; calls bought above force them to BUY as it rises. This is which way the hedging feedback currently runs.

What is measured, and what is assumed. Strikes, open interest, gamma and spot all come off the tape — greeks here are feed-sourced, and spot is solved from put-call parity across 63 strikes rather than taken from a single quote. What is assumed is that dealers are long the calls and short the puts. Nobody publishes dealer inventory; it is inferred from the customer flow it implies. That is the standard convention and a real assumption — when customers have been net sellers of calls, the sign flips and every conclusion above inverts.

Read this as a map of where hedging pressure can build, not a forecast. Max pain says where option buyers lose most, not where price is going, and pinning evidence is strongest only in the final hours and only at strikes with genuinely heavy open interest. Unbacktested in this repo — unlike the trend and rotation systems, no walk-forward test has been run on whether these levels predict anything.
