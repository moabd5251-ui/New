Exposure is a count, not a call. Each market runs 5 or 10 independent trend strategies. Every strategy votes long, short or neutral, and exposure is their net vote as a share of the maximum — nine of ten long reads +90%. Exposure builds as more strategies confirm and bleeds off as they drop out, so a position is never all-in on one signal.

What the book says

One posture explains almost the whole report.

Every equity index is net long and every government-bond market is net short — US 10s and 30s, Bunds and Gilts all sit at the maximum −100%, Canada at −90%, JGBs at −80%. That is a clean higher-yields, risk-on configuration, and it is unanimous: no bond market has a single long strategy anywhere in the report. The dollar is the one place the models disagree with themselves — long USD against the euro and the yen, short USD against the pound and the franc — so read the currency lines as four separate crosses, not one dollar view. Gold is short at −80% while equities rally, the report's sharpest internal tension.

They agree on everything that matters except three things. Across all seven equity indices and both US bond maturities the two books take the same side, and mostly within 20 points — independent model families landing on the same trend is the strongest confirmation either one offers. The disagreements cluster perfectly: gold and both dollar crosses. TrendCompass is short gold at −80% while your ETF model just reversed GLD to +30%; TrendCompass is maximum short the euro while your model is +60% long it. Neither is the correct one, and gold is exactly the market where a futures book and a spot-ETF book can legitimately part company. Treat these three as unresolved rather than as a signal from either side.
