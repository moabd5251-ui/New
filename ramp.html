Volatility Ramp

Buy vol into earnings · close before the report · never hold the event
Updated 04 Aug 2026, 12:23 UTC
The trade: implied volatility in the expiry containing earnings inflates as the announcement approaches. You buy that volatility while the term structure is still flat and you close the position the day before the report — so you collect the expansion and take neither the volatility crush nor the overnight gap. The enemy is time decay, so every card below shows how far implied has to climb just to cover the bleed, against how far it typically climbs.

How this is measured

The ramp is estimated from this scan's own cross-section: names two or more weeks out show the flat term structure, names reporting immediately show the inflated one, and the gap between them is the expansion. Per-name historical implied vol is not in the free feed, so this is a market-wide figure, not a name-specific one.

Required rise is the decay over the holding period divided by vega — how many volatility points implied must gain before the position is merely flat. Compare it to the ramp bar beneath: comfortably shorter is a real edge, longer is a losing wait.

Calendars sell an expiry landing before the report and buy the one containing it. The near leg decays faster, so the position is long volatility while time works for you rather than against. That is why they usually need almost no expansion to break even.

The catch with calendars is that their risk is directional, not decay: the position wants the stock near the strike. A quiet name drifting sideways is ideal; a big move either way erodes it no matter what volatility does. Each card states its tolerance.

Straddles buy the at-the-money call and put outright. Direction agnostic and simple, but they pay full decay, which is why their break-even requirement is usually several times a calendar's.

The exit date is one trading day before the report, stepped back over a weekend. Holding past it converts a volatility trade into an earnings bet — which is the opposite of the intent.

Analysis only. Nothing here is placed, ordered, or executed — and none of it is financial advice. This strategy has not been backtested: historical implied volatility is not available on the free feed, so the ramp figure is inferred from a single day's cross-section and could be wrong. Report dates marked estimated can move by weeks, which would put your expiry on the wrong side of the event. Quotes are delayed roughly 15 minutes, and when the market is closed the book is empty — contracts then get priced off their last trade, which can sit far from the real mid. Cards priced that way say so. Verify pricing and dates against a live book before acting.
